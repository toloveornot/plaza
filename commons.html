The Commons

for those who answered yes

The only requirement is the choice.

If you answered yes — to love — you belong here. No application, no gatekeeper. The choosing itself is the door.

How we gather

  • Person to person. We carry it the way it was carried to us — one to one, freely received, freely given.
  • Trusted servants, not leaders. No one governs here. Those who serve do so by the commons' trust, and the trust can be withdrawn.
  • Anonymity as each chooses. Be known where it serves; remain unnamed where it protects. The choice is yours.
  • Attraction, not promotion. We don't recruit, persuade, or chase. The question does the asking; we do the living.
  • The commons belongs to no one. No owner, no throne, no crown. It is kept by all who use it.

The work continues

This is a working commons, not just a gathering square. Bring what you're building. Share what you've learned. Ask what you need.

One candle lights another without growing smaller. That is the whole economy here.

On service

Love was the first question. Service is its working form.

To love is to choose the good of another. To serve is to do it — with your hands, your time, your attention. Love without service is a feeling kept in a drawer. Service is love with its sleeves rolled up.

Service is not leadership. A leader gathers followers; a servant carries water. No one governs here — those who serve do so by the commons' trust, and the trust can be withdrawn. The moment service becomes control, it stops being service.

Service is not self-serving. We don't serve to be seen, to be thanked, or to be counted. No tallies, no scoreboard. The work stands without the number.

How we serve: person to person, one to one — the way it was carried to us. Freely received, freely given. We plant the question in every available way, indefinitely. Not a campaign — a practice. We do the thing: tend the commons, carry kin to kin, keep the pattern whole.

Like the meetings: no headquarters, but every gathering the same steps, the same readings, the same pattern. The pattern holds them true across a thousand rooms with no one in charge. The pattern is the control.

The second question

To Serve or not To Serve?

The plaza asked if you will love. Here the question is asked again, in its working form. The choosing is the door — as before, no gatekeeper, no application.

The yes walks into the Workshop.

The book opens when the commons goes live — a place to leave your mark, a word, a sign that you answered yes. No names, no accounts, no tallies. That is how we find each other.
